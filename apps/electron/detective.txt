The Man Who Collected Alibis

Detective Mara Reyes didn't believe in perfect witnesses. A perfect witness, in
her experience, was just a liar who had practiced.

So when Gerald Whitmore — tech heir, collector of rare clocks, and the least
sympathetic man in the city — turned up dead in his penthouse with every lock
engaged and every camera dead at exactly 2:14 AM, she ignored the obvious
suspects and went looking for the one person with nothing to say.

That was Alice Tran, the night doorman, who had worked the front desk for nine
years and never once called in sick. When Mara sat down beside him in the
lobby, he didn't look up.

"I heard you spoke to everyone who entered that night," Mara said.

"Yes."

"Everyone except one."

Alice polished the brass button on his blazer. "Mr. Whitmore owned the building.
He didn't enter. He lived here."

Mara unfolded a printout of the service elevator logs. "At 2:11, the freight
elevator went to the penthouse. Nobody signed it out. Security footage shows
nothing in the car — empty, both ways."

"That's right." He finally met her eyes. "Nothing in the car. Just the clock."

"The clock?"

"A grandfather clock. Mr. Whitmore had it delivered to the lobby for repair at
9 PM. It came back at 2:11. Heavy, the repairman said. Took three men." Alice
set the brass button down precisely. "The clocks were his whole life. He was
never seen without them."

Mara went back to the penthouse and stood before the grandfather clock. Its
pendulum had stopped, mid-swing, as if interrupted. She opened the case.

Inside, where the weights should have hung, was a clean empty space the size of
a man who had learned to be very small, and very patient, and very angry.

She checked the repair shop's records. The clock had been serviced by a
watchmaker who retired six years ago — the same year Whitmore's former partner,
a man named Ellis, had been driven out of the company and erased from every
document, every photo, every record that remained.

Ellis, who had no alibi, because he no longer existed.

Mara closed the clock case and let the pendulum rest. Some crimes, she thought,
aren't solved by finding the person with the weakest story. They're solved by
finding the person the story was written to erase.

Outside, the city went on lying at 2:14 AM, and the grandfather clock kept its
silence like a witness who had finally been allowed to speak.
